## State these in the paper

- Labels were assigned blind by Claude (an AI), not by humans. Do not call it human evaluation.
- Pretrained Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct and a pretrained DeBERTa NLI model; nothing was trained or fine-tuned.
- 80 of the 120 evaluation reports come from the train split (never trained on, because nothing was trained); 40 are held-out test reports.
- Verifier-scored numbers for the best-of-N output are circular. Only the labeled rows and the plain-output AUC are independent.
- The checked drafts are not simpler to read. Frame the contribution as meaning-preserving selection, not simplification.
- The omission AUC rests on 5 error cases. Credit BodyParts3D (CC BY 4.0) beside any 3D screenshot.

## Metrics the paper reports

Error rates H (hedge), N (number or side), F (unsupported), O (omission) with 95% paired-bootstrap CIs; AUC per verifier
component against its matching label; reading grade and simplicity; verifier-scored metrics marked circular.

## Gaps a reviewer will notice

1. No human labels or inter-annotator agreement (Cohen's kappa). Even one teammate labeling 40 to 50 items helps (`scripts/score_human_labels.py` exists).
2. No random-pick ablation (best-of-N picked at random vs by the verifier). Uses cached candidates, no new generation. The most useful single addition.
3. No baseline beyond the plain draft, and no patient comprehension study: state as limitations.
